The oldest known written legal code is the Code of Ur-Nammu, dating back to about 2100–2050 BCE in ancient Sumer.
"Habeas corpus" literally translates from Latin as "you shall have the body."
The world's longest trial lasted over 3 years in the Philippines — the trial of former president Joseph Estrada.
International law has no single global police force — it largely relies on treaties, conventions, and mutual agreement.
The International Court of Justice, located in The Hague, was established in 1945 as the principal judicial organ of the United Nations.
The phrase "beyond reasonable doubt" became a criminal law standard in the 18th century.
Roman law is the foundation of many modern legal systems, including civil law traditions across Europe.
The Magna Carta of 1215 is considered one of the earliest documents to limit the power of a monarch.
The Geneva Conventions, first adopted in 1864, establish international legal standards for humanitarian treatment in war.
In many countries, ignorance of the law is not a defence — a principle dating back to Roman times.
