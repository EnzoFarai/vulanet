DNA: Location, Structure and Functions

Location of DNA

DNA is mainly found in two locations, leading to it being categorised as either:

1. Nuclear DNA

  • Present in the nucleus (as nuclear DNA) – makes up genes on chromosomes.

2. Extra-nuclear DNA

  • Present in mitochondria (as mitochondrial DNA).
  • Present in chloroplasts (in plant cells, as chloroplast DNA).

Brief History on the Discovery of the DNA Molecule

During the early 20th century, scientists already knew the chemical composition of DNA and understood that it carried genetic information. However, its three-dimensional structure remained unknown. Understanding its shape was considered essential because the structure would explain how DNA stores information, replicates accurately, and directs protein production. For this reason, many scientists were actively working to determine the true form of the DNA molecule.

The solution eventually came through the combined efforts of several scientists:

  • Rosalind Franklin: An expert in X-ray crystallography. Her clear diffraction image, Photograph 51, provided strong evidence that DNA had a helical (spiral) structure and was crucial for interpreting the molecule's shape.
Figure 1: Photograph 51, the X-ray image of DNA taken by Rosalind Franklin, shows a clear "X" pattern. The angles and pattern of this X, along with other features of the image, provided crucial evidence used to build the first accurate double-helix model.
  • Maurice Wilkins: A colleague of Franklin who also worked with DNA using X-ray techniques. He shared Franklin's data—without her knowledge—with James Watson and Francis Crick.
  • James Watson and Francis Crick: Using Franklin's X-ray data and existing chemical evidence, they built the first accurate model of DNA in 1953. Their model showed DNA as two strands twisted into a double helix, with complementary base pairs: adenine (A) pairing with thymine (T), and guanine (G) pairing with cytosine (C).

The work of all four scientists helped further biology, medicine, and the emerging field of biotechnology. In 1962, for their contributions, Watson, Crick, and Wilkins were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, one of the highest honours in science. Franklin, who had died in 1958, was not included, as the prize is not awarded posthumously (after death).

Figure 2: The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1962 was awarded to Francis Crick, James Watson and Maurice Wilkins for their discovery of the molecular structure of DNA.

Structure of DNA

The natural shape of the DNA molecule is a double helix.

Each strand of the helix is made up of a sequence of monomers (building blocks) called nucleotides.

Figure 3: The Structure of DNA

Components of a DNA Nucleotide

DNA nucleotides consist of three components:

  • Four nitrogenous bases, which are linked by weak hydrogen bonds: The four bases are adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C), and guanine (G).

Base pairing in DNA occurs as follows:

  • Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T)
  • Guanine (G) pairs with Cytosine (C)
  • A sugar portion (known as deoxyribose in DNA)
  • A phosphate group

Functions of DNA

Along with other roles in organisms, DNA mainly performs two key functions:

  • DNA makes up genes which carry hereditary information. These genes pass characteristics from parents to offspring.
  • DNA contains coded information for protein synthesis. This code tells cells how to make proteins, which are essential for growth and proper functioning.
